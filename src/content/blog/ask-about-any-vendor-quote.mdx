say you get two quotes on the same Tuesday. Same guest count, same date, same style of food. One is noticeably lower than the other, so you forward it to your partner with three fire emojis and start mentally spending the difference. Three weeks later you read both contracts side by side and the cheaper one has a service charge sitting underneath the total, a four-hour minimum, and a line about overtime billed in 30-minute increments. The expensive one included all of it up front.

Neither vendor lied to you. The quote is never the whole number. It is a starting point written by someone who wants to look affordable next to the quote you got yesterday, and the only way to compare two of them honestly is to ask the same four questions of both.

Here they are, in the order they tend to cost you money.

## 01. Is service in the total?

The specific version of this question: **is the service charge in the catering total?**

Catering quotes are the single most common place where the number you remember is not the number you pay. A per-person price can be quoted as food only, or food and staffing, or food, staffing, rentals, and bar, and every one of those is technically a "catering quote." Service charge may be listed as a separate line, folded into the per-person number, or mentioned once in a paragraph you skim.

Ask it plainly and ask for the all-in number. Not "does this include everything," which invites a yes. Say: **is the service charge in the catering total, or is it added on top?** Then ask whether tax applies to the service charge too, because in some places it does and in some places it doesn't, and that difference is real money on a catering bill.

While you have them on the phone, the same question applies to:

1. Staffing and bartender fees
2. Cake cutting or corkage
3. Rentals (linens, glassware, chairs) versus what the venue already owns
4. Delivery, setup, and strike
5. Gratuity, and whether gratuity is separate from the service charge or the same thing

That last one trips up a lot of couples. A service charge is not automatically a tip to the staff. Ask which it is so you know whether you are expected to tip on top. ## 02. What does overtime cost?

The specific version: **what happens if the reception runs late?**

Weddings run late. Toasts go long, dinner service slips, the dance floor finally fills at the exact moment someone taps their watch. Overtime is not a failure of planning, it is a normal outcome, and the vendors who have worked a hundred weddings know that. Which is why the overtime rate is in the contract, whether or not anyone mentioned it on the call.

Ask for the rate, and then ask four follow-ups that actually determine what you pay:

1. **Who does overtime apply to?** The venue, the caterer and their staff, the band or DJ, the photographer, and the rental pickup may all bill separately. One late hour can mean several charges, not one.
2. **How is it billed?** Per hour, per half hour, or rounded up to the next full hour. Rounding up is where a ten-minute overrun becomes a full-hour charge.
3. **Who can authorize it?** If your planner, your mom, or the DJ can wave a hand and extend the night, decide in advance who is allowed to say yes.
4. **Is there a hard stop?** Many venues have a noise ordinance or a building curfew that no amount of money overrides. If so, overtime isn't an option, and you need an end-of-night plan instead.

Get the answers in writing before you sign. "We're flexible" is not a rate.

## 03. Is the deposit refundable?

The specific version: **do we get the venue deposit back if we move the date?**

Note what that question is actually asking. Not cancellation, which most couples never need. Moving the date is the thing that really happens: a family health situation, a military posting, a venue double-booking, a construction delay, a visa timeline for guests traveling in.

So ask about three different scenarios, because the contract usually treats them differently:

1. **You cancel outright.** Is any portion of the deposit refundable, and up to what date?
2. **You move the date.** Does the deposit transfer, and to which dates (any date, weekdays only, same calendar year, off-season only)? Is there a transfer or rebooking fee?
3. **They cancel or can't deliver.** What are you owed, and how fast? This clause is often much weaker than the one protecting them.

Also ask what the deposit is called. "Retainer" and "non-refundable deposit" usually mean the money is gone the moment it clears, and that is a perfectly normal industry practice. You just want to know which bucket your money is in before you send it, not after. For more on the clauses worth reading twice, see [contracts](/blog/category/contracts).

## 04. What isn't included?

The specific version: **what's not in the DJ package?**

This is the question that catches everything the first three missed, and it is the one almost nobody asks, because it feels slightly rude. It is not rude. Good vendors answer it immediately and in detail, because they would much rather tell you now than argue about it in week 40.

For a DJ package, "not included" might mean ceremony sound, a second setup for cocktail hour, uplighting, a wireless mic for toasts, travel, or the hours before the reception when they're on site but not playing. For a photographer, it might mean the engagement session, a second shooter, an album, or raw files. For florals, it might mean installation labor, hard goods you're renting rather than buying, and breakdown at the end of the night.

Phrase it as an open question: **what isn't included in this package that couples usually end up adding?** That wording invites an honest list instead of a defensive one.

## Watch for these red flags

- **A quote with no itemization.** One number and no line items means you can't compare it to anything, and you can't tell what gets cut if you need to trim.
- **"Don't worry about that part."** Any version of this about a fee, a minimum, or a date change is a reason to slow down.
- **Verbal promises that never reach the contract.** If they said they'd throw in the extra hour, it belongs in writing. Kind people forget things. Staff turns over.
- **Pressure to send a deposit before you've seen the full agreement.** A hold is reasonable. Money before terms is not.
- **A different number every time you ask.** If the total moves between the email, the call, and the PDF, ask for one final document and work from that only.
- **Vague overtime language.** "Billed at our standard rate" with no rate stated is a blank line in your budget.
