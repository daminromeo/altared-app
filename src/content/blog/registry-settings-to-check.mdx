a couple finished their registry on a tuesday night, took a screenshot of the link, and dropped it into the save-the-date file their stationer was waiting on. ninety-one gifts got purchased over the next four months. then they moved apartments three weeks after the wedding, tried to return a duplicate blender, and found out two things at once: the return window on their account had never been switched on, and eleven boxes had shipped to the old address because nobody ever updated it.

none of that was a mistake in the registry itself. the registry was fine. the problem lived in the settings tab, which almost nobody opens, because the front of the page looks finished the moment you've added enough items.

the registry link looks ready. but four settings can quietly cost you money or create headaches once guests start buying. completion discounts, return windows, shipping addresses, and cash fund fees. none of these show up on the front of the page.

check all four before you drop the link in the save-the-date. takes ten minutes and it matters a lot more than picking the font on the invite.

## 01. the completion discount

this is the one with actual dollars attached, and it's the one people find out about after it's expired.

many stores offer 10-20% off remaining items after the date, but only for a limited window. the idea is simple: whatever didn't get purchased off your list, you can buy yourself at a discount once the wedding is over. it's how retailers keep you shopping with them instead of walking away with a half-finished kitchen.

the catch is that the window is short and it's tied to your event date, which means the clock starts whether or not you're paying attention. if you're on a honeymoon, then unpacking, then dealing with thank-you notes, you can lose the whole thing without ever seeing a notice about it.

### what to actually check

1. **is a completion discount offered at all?** not every retailer has one. if it's a major department store or big-box home retailer, usually yes. smaller boutiques and some online-only shops, often no.
2. **what's the percentage?** the range you'll typically see is 10-20% off remaining items. know your number so you can plan around it.
3. **how long is the window and when does it start?** some start on your event date. some start the day after. some run from a fixed number of days before the wedding through a number of days after.
4. **does the event date on file match your actual wedding date?** this is the quiet killer. if you set up the registry early with a placeholder date, or you moved the wedding, the discount window may be calculated off the wrong day.
5. **does it apply to everything or only registry items?** sometimes it's limited to items still on your list, sometimes it extends to the whole store, sometimes certain brands and appliances are excluded.

### why the math is worth ten minutes

say you registered for a full set of cookware, a few small appliances, and the good towels, and about $1,800 of it goes unpurchased, which is extremely normal. a 20% completion discount on that is $360 you keep. at 10% it's $180. either way, that's a real line item, and it's money you only get by knowing the window exists and putting it on your calendar.

put the discount end date in your phone the day you finish the registry. not after the wedding. you will not remember after the wedding.

## 02. the return window

registry gifts often get a longer return period, sometimes 1 year. but only if the setting is on.

this one surprises people because the extended return period feels like it should be automatic. it isn't always. on some platforms it's an opt-in toggle in your registry preferences. on others it's tied to whether the gift was purchased through the registry link versus purchased off-list and brought to the gift table.

the standard retail return window might be 30 to 90 days. a registry return window can stretch to a year, which matters enormously for wedding gifts, because you often don't unbox everything until you've moved, and you don't discover the duplicate stand mixer until month five.

### the parts people miss

- **gift receipts.** some platforms attach them automatically to registry purchases, some don't. if yours doesn't, every return becomes a conversation.
- **off-registry gifts don't get the extension.** a guest who buys the same item from a different store is giving you a 30-day return window, not a one-year one. this is a quiet argument for keeping your registry list current so guests aren't improvising.
- **returns vs. exchanges.** a few retailers will let you exchange well past the point where they'll give you a refund. read which one you've actually got.
- **store credit only.** some extended registry returns pay out in store credit, not to the original payment method. that's still useful, but it changes how you think about a duplicate.

## 03. the shipping address

if you're moving after the wedding, update this before guests start buying. re-routing is a hassle.

there is no elegant fix for this one once it's in motion. a package that has already shipped to an old address is a carrier problem, a former-landlord problem, and sometimes a lost-gift problem. and the worst version is the one where it's a $300 gift from your aunt and you have to tell her you never got it.

### how to set it up so this doesn't happen

decide on a single shipping address before you share the link, and make it the address you will reliably have access to for the next twelve months. for a lot of couples that's:

1. the apartment you're staying in through the wedding, if you're not moving for at least six months after
2. one set of parents' house, if you're moving and the timing is uncertain
3. the new place, if the lease starts before invitations go out and you're confident in the date

also worth checking in the same visit: whether the platform lets guests ship directly to you or holds everything for a single consolidated delivery, and whether your address is visible to guests on the public page. some couples are fine with that, some aren't. it's a setting either way.

and if you do move mid-registry, don't just change the default. check whether orders already placed but not yet shipped will pick up the new address or keep the old one. the answer varies by retailer and it's usually buried in a help article.

## 04. the cash fund fee

some platforms take a fee on each cash gift, some waive it. check the payout settings first.

cash funds and honeymoon funds have become a normal, unembarrassing part of registries, which is good. what's less visible is that the money doesn't always arrive whole. a platform may charge a percentage on each gift, or pass along a card processing fee, or offer a choice: the couple absorbs the fee, or the guest is given the option to cover it at checkout.

that last option is the one to look at closely, because the default setting decides who pays. if the default has the guest covering it, your guest sees a higher total than the gift amount they chose. if the default has you covering it, a $200 gift lands as something less than $200.

### work out the numbers before you decide

if you expect $4,000 in cash gifts and the platform takes 2.5%, that's $100. at 5%, it's $200. not catastrophic, but it's a rehearsal-dinner centerpiece order, and it's worth knowing which way you've chosen rather than discovering it on the payout statement.

while you're in there, confirm:

- **the payout method and timing.** direct deposit versus a mailed check, and whether funds release as gifts come in or in a lump after the wedding.
- **the bank account on file.** especially if either of you is changing a name or opening a joint account.
- **whether fees are listed on the guest-facing page.** transparency here saves you an awkward text later.

## red flags to watch for

- **a placeholder event date you never updated.** it silently moves your completion discount window and sometimes your return window too.
- **no fee disclosure anywhere on the cash fund page.** if a platform won't tell you the fee before you create the fund, assume there is one and go find it in the terms.
- **"free registry" messaging with a fee buried in payouts.** free to create is not the same as free to receive.
- **a shipping address auto-filled from an old account.** if you set up a shopping account in college, that's probably the address sitting there.
- **a cash fund that pays out only to one person's account** when you've both been telling guests it's a joint gift. fix that early, not in month three.
- **duplicate registries at two retailers with conflicting addresses.** pick your shipping address once and apply it everywhere.

## the ten-minute pass

do this in one sitting, before the link goes anywhere:

1. open the settings tab on every registry you've created, not just the main one
2. confirm your event date is correct, then find the completion discount percentage and end date and put that date in your calendar
3. turn on the extended return window if it's a toggle, and check whether gift receipts attach automatically
4. set the shipping address you'll still have access to in twelve months, and check whether it's publicly visible
5. read the cash fund fee, decide who absorbs it, and confirm the payout account and timing
6. place one small test purchase yourself if the platform allows it, so you see exactly what a guest sees

then share the link.
