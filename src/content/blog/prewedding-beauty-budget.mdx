you get a quote from a hair and makeup artist: $800 for the bride, day-of. it feels clean. you write $800 in the budget spreadsheet, you feel organized, you move on to linens.

then eleven months later you add it all up. the facials you started in january. the hair trial that was billed separately. the makeup trial that was also billed separately. the whitening treatment you started in the spring because your dentist said it takes months. the $160 in cash you pulled out of an ATM at 7am on the wedding morning because the artist's Venmo wasn't working.

the real number was never $800.

bridal beauty is one of the most underbudgeted wedding categories, because the costs start 6 to 12 months out and almost no one tracks them as a group. they arrive one at a time, in different months, from different providers, on different invoices. individually each one feels small and reasonable. together they can quietly add $800 to $2,500 to your beauty spend before the wedding morning even starts.

here are the five that show up earliest, what they actually cost, and how to get them into your budget now instead of discovering them in pieces.

## 01. skin prep: $300 to $1,200+, starting 6 to 12 months out

this is the first beauty expense almost every bride has, and it's the one that almost never gets labeled as a wedding expense. it just gets labeled as "skincare."

a full treatment plan runs 6-12 months and $300-$1,200+ before booking anyone. that's the range whether you're doing a monthly facial, a series of chemical peels, microneedling, a dermatologist-prescribed routine, or some combination. the reason it starts so early isn't vanity, it's biology. most treatments that actually change your skin need multiple sessions spaced weeks apart, plus a buffer so you're not peeling or purging in your photos. anything aggressive needs to be finished, not in progress, about a month out.

### what pushes this range around

- **monthly facials** at $100 to $175 a session for six months lands you squarely mid-range
- **a peel or microneedling series** is usually sold as a package of three to six, and the package price is where you cross $800 fast
- **prescription routines** (tretinoin, hydroquinone, anything from a derm) add visit fees plus ongoing product costs
- **product, not just service.** the cleanser and serum your esthetician recommends is often $200 to $400 across the whole runway, and nobody counts it

the honest version: decide what you're doing before you start, price the full series (not the first session), and put the whole number in your budget on day one. a $150 facial that you will get eight times is a $1,200 line item, not a $150 line item.

## 02. the hair trial: $100 to $250, billed separately

this is where the "my quote was $800" math starts falling apart.

trials are billed separately, usually $100-$250, and most stylists require one before day-of. that requirement is reasonable. the stylist needs to see your hair's texture, length, how it holds, whether extensions are needed, how your veil sits. you need to see whether the vision in your saved folder works on your actual head. it's a good use of money. it's just not free, and it's usually not in the package price.

a few things to confirm in writing before you book:

1. **is the trial price separate from, or credited toward, the day-of rate?** some artists credit it. most don't. ask directly.
2. **how many looks does the trial cover?** one look, or two (ceremony and reception)? a second look is often another $75 to $150.
3. **is there a travel fee for the trial?** if the trial is at your home or a venue-adjacent location, there can be a mileage or minimum charge on top.
4. **what happens if you want a second trial?** many brides do. the second one is almost always full price.
5. **does the trial date lock your booking?** some stylists hold your date only after the trial and retainer both clear.

if you're comparing two stylists, compare the trial too. a $750 day-of rate with a $250 trial is $1,000. a $850 day-of rate with a $100 trial is $950. the cheaper headline number is the more expensive booking.

## 03. the makeup trial: $100 to $300, also separate

another separate line item, $100-$300, rarely included in the day-of package price.

makeup trials run slightly higher than hair trials on the top end because of product usage and because many artists build in a longer appointment to test foundation matching, lash options, and how everything photographs. if your artist does a flash test (taking photos with flash to check for flashback from SPF or certain foundations), that's worth paying for. it's the difference between looking great in the mirror and looking great in the gallery you'll have forever.

if you're booking hair and makeup from the same team or salon, ask whether there's a combined trial rate. sometimes there is, sometimes it's just two invoices on the same day. either way, plan on $200 to $550 for both trials together, on a date that is often three to five months before the wedding.

### the trial cost people forget entirely

the trial day itself has costs around it. if you're doing your trial the same day as engagement photos or a bridal shower (smart, you get use out of the look), fine. if you're taking a half day off work and driving 45 minutes each way, that's real too. not a line item exactly, but worth knowing before you schedule three trials with three different artists.

## 04. professional teeth whitening: $300 to $650, needs 3 to 6 months

professional whitening needs 3-6 months to finish, costs $300-$650, and is almost never budgeted.

this one surprises people the most because whitening feels like a drugstore purchase. professional whitening is a different thing. in-office sessions plus custom trays plus at-home maintenance gel is a program, not an appointment, and dentists space it out specifically to avoid sensitivity. if you start eight weeks before the wedding and try to compress it, you get sensitivity, uneven results, or both.

the timeline matters as much as the money. if you're twelve months out, this is a thing you can decide about later. if you're five months out and you want it, you need to book the consult this month.

worth knowing: custom trays alone often run $200 to $400, and the refill gel is an ongoing cost after that. if you have veneers, crowns, or bonding on your front teeth, whitening won't change them, and your dentist may recommend a different (more expensive) route. ask before you pay for trays.

## 05. the tip envelope: 15 to 20%, in cash, on the morning

tipping hair and makeup 15-20% is standard, on a $800 package that is $120-$160 cash, day-of.

this is the one that catches people at the worst possible moment, because it's the only expense on this list that has to exist as physical cash in a labeled envelope at 7am while someone is steaming your dress. you cannot solve it that morning.

a few notes on how tips actually scale:

- tip on the **total service cost**, not just your own service. if your package covers you plus five bridesmaids and two moms, the tip base is the full invoice.
- if the artist is a **salon owner**, tipping is often optional by convention, but plenty of couples still tip. decide in advance rather than debating it in the chair.
- **assistants** are usually tipped separately, in their own envelopes, typically $20 to $50 each.
- **travel fees and early-start fees** are generally not tipped on, since those are reimbursements, not service.

write the names on the envelopes the week before and hand the whole stack to whoever is running your morning. it's a five-minute task that removes a legitimately stressful moment.

## red flags to watch for when you're getting beauty quotes

these are the patterns that turn a clear quote into a surprise invoice:

- **"we'll figure out the trial later."** if the trial price isn't in writing, assume it's the top of the range.
- **a package price with no itemized breakdown.** you can't compare two vendors if one number is hiding four services.
- **no early-start fee disclosed.** many artists charge extra for start times before 7 or 8am. if your photographer wants you ready by 10 for a large party, you may be triggering it without knowing.
- **no stated per-person rate for the party.** "bridesmaids from $95" means someone's is going to be $140.
- **travel and parking left vague.** downtown venue parking and mileage can add $50 to $200 quietly.
- **a skincare package sold as a "bridal program" with no per-session pricing.** you should be able to see what each session costs and cancel the rest.
- **retainer terms you haven't read.** if you cancel or downsize your party, what's refundable? get it in writing. more on this in our [contracts](/blog/category/contracts) coverage.

## put it in one place before it accumulates

none of these five costs are unreasonable. skin prep works. trials prevent wedding morning panic. whitening shows up in every close-up photo. tips are how you take care of people who got up at 5am for you. the problem is never the individual expense, it's that they arrive across a year from five different providers and never get added together.

so add them together now:

1. **price the full skin prep series, not the first session.** $300-$1,200+ is the honest range. write the total.
2. **ask every hair stylist whether the $100-$250 trial is separate or credited.** get it in writing.
3. **budget $100-$300 for the makeup trial** as its own line, and ask about a combined hair-and-makeup trial rate.
4. **decide on whitening by the 6-month mark.** $300-$650, and the timeline is the constraint, not the money.
5. **pull tip cash the week before.** on a $800 package, that is $120-$160, plus $20-$50 per assistant.
6. **track all of it as one category called "beauty,"** not scattered across "personal," "health," and "hair."

then compare your real number against your original quote. if the gap is $800 to $2,500, you're normal. you're just no longer surprised.
