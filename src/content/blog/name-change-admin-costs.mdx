You get home from the honeymoon on a Sunday. Monday you go to add your new last name to your work email, and someone in HR says they need a certified copy of the marriage certificate. Not a photocopy. Not a scan. An original with a raised seal. You have one. You sent it to the passport agency three weeks ago and you are not getting it back for another month.

That is how the name change process actually goes. It is free in theory and quietly expensive in practice. Nobody quotes you a number for it because it is not a vendor line item, so it never makes it onto the budget spreadsheet. Then you spend a couple hundred dollars in $15 increments over three months and wonder where it went.

Here is every real cost, every real wait time, and the one thing you should absolutely not pay for.

## the six costs nobody quotes you

Total out of pocket ranges from roughly **$200-$500** depending on how fast you need your passport and how many certified copies you order. Timeline from license to every document updated is typically **2-4 months** if you start right after the wedding.

Here is where that money goes, in the order you will actually spend it.

### 1. the marriage license: $10-$115

This one you pay before the wedding, which is why it rarely gets counted as a name change cost. But it is the document the entire chain depends on, and the price swings wildly. The marriage license alone runs **$10-$115 depending on your county**. Same state, two counties over, can be a $60 difference.

A few things worth knowing before you show up at the clerk's office:

- Many counties are cash or money order only. Bring both.
- Some states have a waiting period between the license being issued and the ceremony (often 24 to 72 hours), and some have an expiration window (often 30 to 90 days). Getting the license too early is as much of a problem as getting it too late.
- Some counties discount the fee if you complete a premarital counseling course. It is worth one search before you pay.

### 2. certified copies: $10-$20 each, order at least 6

This is the cost that surprises people most, and it is the one most worth planning for. Certified copies run **$10-$20 each**, and you want **at least 6 of them**, because every agency wants an original.

Six sounds excessive until you start mailing them out. Social Security wants one. The passport agency wants one and holds it for weeks. The DMV wants to see one in person. Your bank, your mortgage servicer, HR, your insurance carrier, the retirement account you forgot about from your first job. Some will hand it back. Some will keep it. Some will mail it back eventually, in an envelope you will not recognize.

At $15 each, six copies is $90. Ordering them all at once with the license is almost always cheaper and faster than going back for one more three separate times, because reorders usually mean a new request fee plus shipping plus a two to four week turnaround.

### 3. passport update: $130 standard, $200 expedited

Your passport is **$130 standard or $200 if you expedite**, and it still takes **6-8 weeks after you mail the paperwork**. Expediting shortens the processing, not the mailing, and not the part where you have to be without your passport and one certified copy for over a month.

There is one exception worth checking: if your current passport was issued less than a year ago, the name correction is typically free (you still pay for shipping). Past that window, you are paying the full renewal fee.

The scheduling problem is real. If you have an international trip on the calendar within four months of the wedding, do not start the passport name change. Travel under your current passport name and book your tickets in that name. Airlines will not care that you got married, they will care that the ticket does not match the document.

### 4. social security: free to file, 10-14 business days to wait

Social Security is genuinely free to file. No fee, no upsell, no catch. You can do it by mail or in person at a field office.

The cost here is time, not money. The card takes **10-14 business days to arrive**, and you should budget two weeks for it before you move on to the DMV. This is the step that sets the pace for everything after it, because most DMVs will not process a Real ID name change until the Social Security Administration's records reflect the new name. Filing here first and then waiting is not optional sequencing, it is the whole reason the timeline runs 2-4 months instead of two weeks.

### 5. real id update: $20-$40 and a 90 minute line

The Real ID update runs **$20-$40** at the DMV. In-person only, no exceptions, no online option, because they have to verify your documents and take a new photo. Lines average **90 minutes**.

Bring more than you think you need: your current license, a certified copy of the marriage certificate, your new Social Security card, and two proofs of address in your new name if you changed those too. Getting turned away for one missing document means doing the 90 minutes again.

Book the earliest appointment slot of the day if your DMV offers appointments. The 90 minute average is an average, and the tail end of it lives in the late afternoon.

### 6. paid name change services: $30-$150 you do not need to spend

If you see a paid name-change service advertising online, skip it. They charge **$30-$150 to send you the same government forms you can download for nothing**.

That is the entire product. A packet of federal and state forms, pre-filled with information you typed into their website, plus a checklist. The forms are free on the Social Security, State Department, and your state DMV websites. The checklist is this article.

These services cannot skip a line for you, cannot expedite your passport, and cannot make the DMV accept a scan instead of an original. They are selling the appearance of a shortcut in a process that does not have one.

## watch for these

A few things that quietly cost people extra money or an extra month:

- **Ordering only one or two certified copies.** The most common and most expensive mistake. A reorder costs you the fee plus shipping plus weeks of waiting while a different agency sits on the copy you have.
- **Going to the DMV before your Social Security card arrives.** You will be turned away, and you will have spent 90 minutes to learn that.
- **Starting the passport change with travel on the books.** 6-8 weeks is the quoted window, not a guarantee, and peak season runs longer.
- **Any service that asks to be paid to file with Social Security.** SSA never charges for a name change. A fee attached to that specific step is the clearest signal you are on the wrong website.
- **Forgetting the accounts that do not remind you.** Passport, license, and Social Security get all the attention. Your car title, professional license, voter registration, deed, will, and beneficiary designations will not send you a notice, and a mismatch on a beneficiary form is the kind of thing that matters at the worst possible time.
- **Assuming your spouse's employer will handle payroll automatically.** Payroll and benefits are usually two separate updates, and the second one is the one tied to your health insurance card.

## a sequence that actually works

The order matters more than the speed. Do it in this order and nothing bounces:

1. **Before the wedding:** get the marriage license ($10-$115) and note your county's waiting period and expiration window.
2. **Week 1 after the wedding:** order at least 6 certified copies at $10-$20 each. Order them all at once.
3. **Week 1-2:** file with Social Security. Free. Then wait 10-14 business days for the card.
4. **Week 3-4:** once the new card is in hand, go to the DMV for the Real ID update ($20-$40, budget 90 minutes).
5. **Week 4:** mail the passport application ($130 standard, $200 expedited) and plan to be without it for 6-8 weeks.
6. **Weeks 4-8:** work through banks, credit cards, employer payroll and benefits, insurance, mortgage or lease, investment and retirement accounts, and the utility bills.
7. **Weeks 8-16:** the long tail. Voter registration, professional licenses, car title and registration, estate documents, beneficiary designations, loyalty and travel programs.

## what to actually budget

Call it **$200-$500** and put it in the spreadsheet before the wedding, not after. On the low end that is a cheap county license, six copies at $10, a standard passport, and a $20 DMV fee. On the high end it is a $115 license, eight copies at $20, an expedited passport at $200, and a $40 Real ID.

The difference between the two ends is almost entirely two decisions: how fast you need your passport, and how many certified copies you order. One of those is worth spending on. The other is worth over-ordering on, because running out costs more than buying two extra.

Quick version to save:

- Marriage license: **$10-$115**, paid before the wedding, varies by county
- Certified copies: **$10-$20 each**, order **at least 6** in one batch
- Social Security: **free**, then wait **10-14 business days**
- Real ID: **$20-$40**, in person only, **90 minute** average wait
- Passport: **$130** standard or **$200** expedited, **6-8 weeks** either way
- Paid name change services: **$30-$150** for free forms. Skip them.
- Total: roughly **$200-$500** over **2-4 months**
