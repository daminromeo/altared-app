You get the quote back and it says $3,200. You screenshot it, send it to your partner with a "this feels doable," and mentally cross photography off the stress list. Three weeks later the actual contract lands in your inbox and the number at the bottom is $4,900. Nothing was misrepresented. You just didn't know the base price was only the base.

This happens constantly, and not because photographers are sneaky. Photography packages are built a la carte, and most couples don't know which pieces are missing from the headline number until the itemized version shows up. By then you've already fallen for the portfolio, done three email exchanges, and it feels awkward to renegotiate.

So here are the four add-ons that quietly inflate the quote. Learn them now, before you book, and you'll read every photography quote a lot faster.

## 1. The second shooter

A second shooter is a second photographer who works your day alongside the lead. They cover the angles one person physically cannot: the groom's side while the lead is with the bride, the guests' faces during your first look, the wide room shot while the lead gets the tight one.

Most couples assume the price they were quoted includes a full team. It usually doesn't. A second shooter is **billed separately, usually $400 to $900 extra**, depending on the market and how many hours they're on.

Whether you need one comes down to your day:

1. **Getting ready in two locations.** If you and your partner are prepping across town from each other, one photographer can't be in both places. A second shooter earns their fee here.
2. **A guest count over roughly 120.** Big receptions have too much happening at once for one camera.
3. **A tight timeline.** If your ceremony-to-reception window is tiny, two shooters buy you speed on formal portraits.
4. **A small, simple day.** Intimate ceremony, one venue, under 60 guests? You may genuinely not need one, and that's $400 to $900 back in your pocket.

Ask directly: "Is a second shooter included, and if not, what's the add-on price?" Don't assume the answer from the package name.

## 2. The engagement session

The engagement session is the one couples are most surprised to find isn't standard. It reads like a warm-up, a chance to get comfortable in front of the camera before the wedding, and a lot of couples assume it comes with the package.

It's **not included by default, and it adds $250 to $600.**

Here's the honest case for it. An engagement session is the only time before the wedding you'll actually work with your photographer, so you learn how they direct you and they learn how you move. That rehearsal is real, and the photos give you material for save-the-dates and a guest book. If those things matter to you, the fee is fair.

The honest case against it: if you're tight on budget and you're confident in front of a camera, this is one of the easiest line items to cut. It has zero impact on your wedding-day coverage. Some photographers will also fold a discounted session in if you ask while negotiating the full package, so it's worth raising before you sign rather than after.

## 3. The heirloom print album

This is the one that surprises couples the most on price. You picture a nice photo book. What photographers are quoting is a hand-bound, thick-page, archival album, and **heirloom albums run $800 to $2,000 and rarely ship free.**

Two things inflate this line:

- **The album itself.** Cover material, page count, and print quality all move the number. A 30-page linen album is not the same product as a 10-page starter book, and packages that mention "an album" rarely tell you which one.
- **Shipping and design.** These albums are custom-built objects, so the base price rarely includes free delivery, and some studios charge a design fee on top for laying out the spreads.

You have real options here, and they're worth knowing:

1. **Skip the physical album at the wedding stage.** You keep the digital files (confirm you're getting high-resolution files with print rights) and order an album later once the cash flow relaxes.
2. **Downgrade the size.** A smaller album at a lower page count can drop you toward the bottom of that $800 to $2,000 range instead of the top.
3. **Buy it as an anniversary purchase.** Nothing forces you to order the album inside the wedding contract. Some couples wait a year and treat it as a first-anniversary gift.

The album is often the single most negotiable and most cuttable big-ticket add-on in a photography quote, precisely because it isn't tied to the coverage itself. It's almost never in the base price, so budget for it as a separate decision.

## 4. The travel fee

The travel fee is the sneakiest of the four because it's tied to a distance most couples never think to measure. The common trigger: **anything over 30 miles from the photographer's home base gets billed.** Your venue can feel local and still cross that line, especially if your photographer is based in a different suburb than your reception.

How it's charged varies, and the two formats can produce very different numbers:

- **Per mile:** sometimes **$0.70 per mile**. On a venue that's 80 miles away, round trip, that math climbs quietly.
- **Flat fee:** sometimes **a flat $300**, regardless of exact distance, once you're outside the radius.

Ask two questions: "Where do you measure from?" and "What's the fee structure past your radius?" And if your wedding is a true destination, travel gets its own category entirely (flights, hotel nights, sometimes a day-rate for travel time). Get all of that in writing before you sign, not verbally.

## Watch for these red flags when you compare quotes

The add-ons themselves are normal. What you're screening for is a quote that hides how big it can grow. Watch for:

- **A package name that implies completeness without listing what's in it.** "The Full Day Collection" tells you nothing about second shooters or albums. Make them itemize.
- **The phrase "starting at."** That's a floor, not a price. Ask what the realistic all-in looks like for a day like yours.
- **"Album included" with no size, page count, or cover spec.** A vague album line is where a $2,000 upgrade lives.
- **No travel policy mentioned at all.** Silence isn't "free." It usually means the fee gets added once they map your venue.
- **A verbal "don't worry, we'll figure travel out later."** Later is after you've signed and lost leverage. Pin it down now.

None of these mean the photographer is dishonest. Remember, it's not hidden on purpose. It's just that photography packages are built a la carte, and the quote is written to look appealing, not complete.

## Run the real math before you fall in love with a number

Take that $3,200 headline quote from the top of this post. Now stack the add-ons at the low end of each range: a second shooter at $400, an engagement session at $250, a print album at $800, and a flat $300 travel fee. That's $1,750 on top, and you're at $4,950 for the exact same photographer. At the high end of each range, the gap is far wider.

That's not a reason to panic. It's a reason to compare quotes on the same basis. When you're weighing two photographers, put them side by side with every add-on filled in, because the one with the lower base price can easily end up more expensive once the a la carte pieces are added. ## Before you sign, do this

- **Ask if a second shooter is included** and get the add-on price in writing ($400 to $900 range).
- **Confirm whether the engagement session is in the package** or a $250 to $600 extra you can cut.
- **Get the album spec:** size, page count, cover, shipping, and design fee, and know it can run $800 to $2,000.
- **Ask where they measure travel from** and whether it's per-mile ($0.70/mile) or a flat fee ($300) past 30 miles.
