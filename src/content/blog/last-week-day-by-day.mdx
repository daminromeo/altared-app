say it's monday, your wedding is saturday, and three things land in your inbox at once: the caterer wants a locked number, the venue is asking when the seating file is coming, and your aunt has texted "what time do you want me there?" for the third time. none of those are hard questions. they are only hard because they all arrived while you were trying to pick up a dress.

the last week is not the time to improvise. it is the time to send six specific things, in order, so that by friday night the only job left is showing up. here is the week, one day at a time, with the exact lines you can copy and send.

## day 1: final count

call your caterer with the locked guest count. most require 72 hours notice for any change, so doing this on day one gives you buffer instead of a scramble.

"locked" means you have stopped chasing. the cousin who hasn't replied is a no. the plus-one who said "probably" is a no unless they confirm today. count your vendors too if your contract says they're fed, and count kids separately if your contract prices them separately.

### before you call, have these ready

1. your final adult count
2. your kids count, if priced differently
3. your vendor meal count
4. any allergy or dietary notes with the guest's name attached
5. your table count, because the caterer will ask

call, don't email. then send a short email after the call confirming the number in writing. you want a paper trail if the final invoice looks different from what you said out loud.

## day 2: seating chart

send the final file to your venue contact today, not the day before. the venue needs it printed or staged by tomorrow, and "tomorrow" is not a thing you want to discover on thursday.

send it as a file that won't move on them (a PDF, not a shared doc you might still be editing), and name it clearly with your last names and the date. include a table-count summary at the top so nobody has to count seats off a diagram.

if someone drops out after you send it, that's fine. send one follow-up email with the change clearly stated: "removing 2 from table 7, everything else unchanged." what you want to avoid is sending five versions and letting the venue guess which one is current.

## day 3: vendor tips

prep labeled envelopes now. hand them to your point person, not yourself. you will not have a free hand that day.

write the vendor's name and company on the outside of each one. if you are tipping in cash, get it from the bank early in the week, not on friday afternoon. check your contracts first, because some already include a service charge or gratuity, and you don't want to double up or skip someone by accident. if you're unsure which of your contracts already covers it, that's worth a read-through this week. more on reading the fine print in [contracts](/blog/category/contracts).

give your point person a simple list: who gets an envelope, and when. end of the night for some, at load-out for others.

## day 4: run of show

text every vendor their personal call time and arrival spot. not the group timeline. their line.

say: "your load-in is 2pm, side entrance, ask for [name]."

that one sentence kills most of the day-of phone calls before they happen. a vendor who knows the door, the time and the human to ask for does not call you from the parking lot.

### what each vendor text should contain

1. their exact call time
2. the exact entrance or load-in spot
3. the name of the person to ask for on arrival
4. the day-of contact's name and number (not yours)
5. anything specific to them: power access, a getting-ready room, a ceremony cue

ask each one to reply "confirmed." silence is not confirmation. if someone hasn't replied by day 5, call them.

## day 5: family roles

text each family reader, usher or escort their exact job. people want to help and are often too polite to ask what, exactly, they're supposed to do.

say: "you seat guests on the left, start at 3:45."

one text, one person, one job. don't put it in a group chat where everyone reads it and nobody owns it. include where they should stand, when they should move, and who taps them if plans change.

if someone has a reading, send them the actual text of the reading again, even if you already did. and tell them where to sit after.

## day 6: day-of contact

give one person every vendor number and a printed timeline. all vendor questions go to them, not to you.

that last one is the most important thing on this list. if vendor questions go to you on the day, you will spend the morning on your phone. give someone a printed sheet and let them handle it.

printed, on paper. phones die, venues have dead zones, and the person holding the timeline should be able to hand it to someone else mid-afternoon without unlocking anything. put every vendor's name, company, cell number, call time and arrival spot on it, plus the venue contact and the officiant.

then tell your vendors, in the same text from day 4, that this person is the contact. a day-of contact nobody knows about does not stop a single phone call.

## watch for these in the final week

- **a vendor who hasn't confirmed their call time by day 5.** not a crisis yet, but call them, don't text again.
- **a venue that still hasn't acknowledged the seating file.** assume it's not loaded until someone says it is.
- **"we'll figure it out on the day."** from any vendor, that is a sentence to turn into a specific time and a specific door, in writing, today.
- **a contract detail nobody has mentioned all week**, like a load-out deadline or an overtime trigger. reread the terms you signed before the week gets loud.
- **you being the number on every vendor's phone.** if your cell is the only contact in five contracts, send the day-of contact's number to all of them now.
- **cash you planned to get "sometime this week."** banks have hours. friday afternoon is not a plan.

## the week, in one list

1. **final count** to the caterer, locked, and most require 72 hours notice for any change
2. **seating chart** to the venue contact today, as a final file, not the day before
3. **tip envelopes** labeled and handed to your point person, not carried by you
4. **run-of-show texts** to every vendor: call time, arrival spot, who to ask for
5. **individual role texts** to every family usher, reader and escort with their exact job and start time
6. **one person** holding a printed timeline with every vendor number in it

do these in order and the last 48 hours get quiet, which is the actual goal. if your vendor details are still scattered across email threads, screenshots and one group chat, pull them into one place before the week starts. you can [add your quotes free](/get-started) and keep every vendor detail, timeline and contact together, which makes building that printed sheet a ten-minute job instead of an evening.

save this before you need it, then send it to the friend who just got engaged or who is counting down right now.
