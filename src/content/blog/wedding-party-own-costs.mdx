your friend opens the box. tissue paper, a little card, a mini bottle of something. "will you be my bridesmaid?" she screenshots it, posts it, cries a bit, says yes immediately and means it.

what she has actually just agreed to, if you add it all up honestly, is somewhere in the range of $1,400 to $2,500 and roughly a week of her paid time off.

none of that is printed on the card.

this isn't about guilt. most people in your party will say yes and mean it, and they'll be glad they did. but there's a real difference between asking someone to spend that money without knowing, and asking them after you've done the math and made some deliberate choices about what you're covering, what you're skipping, and what you're not going to let spiral. the first one costs friendships. the second one is just good hosting.

here are the six costs your wedding party is quietly absorbing for you, with real numbers.

## the six line items nobody puts in the proposal box

1. **attire plus alterations.** the dress runs $150-300, and alterations add $50-200 on top. call it $150-500 total.
2. **shoes and accessories.** coordinated shoes plus jewelry lands around $80-150.
3. **travel and accommodation.** flights plus hotel for a destination weekend can hit $800+.
4. **shower and bachelorette.** each bridesmaid's share averages $200-400 per event.
5. **the gifts.** a wedding gift plus a bridal shower gift is easily $150-250.
6. **time off.** fittings, rehearsal, wedding day. that's 3-5 days of PTO.

now the detail, because the ranges hide where the pain actually lands.

### 01. attire plus alterations

the number you quote your friends is almost always the dress price. $180, say, from a bridesmaid line that comes in the right color. what nobody mentions is that bridesmaid dresses are cut for a size chart, not a person, and virtually everyone needs work done. hem, straps, bust, waist. alterations add $50-200 on top, and the person who needs the most work (someone petite, someone tall, someone who is pregnant by the time your wedding rolls around) pays the most.

so the honest figure is $150-500 total, and it is not evenly distributed. one bridesmaid pays $200 and another pays $470 for the exact same dress in the exact same photo.

**what you can do:** pick a line with a real size range and a forgiving cut. let people choose their own silhouette in one color rather than one dress in five bodies. if you can cover one thing, cover this one, it's the cost people resent most because they'll never wear the dress again.

### 02. shoes and accessories

coordinated shoes plus jewelry lands around $80-150. this is the cost that feels smallest and annoys people most, because it is almost always avoidable. nude heels in a specific shade, earrings that match the other five sets, a wrap for the ceremony because the venue is cold.

**what you can do:** the fastest $100 you can give back to every single person in your party is to say "wear any shoes you own, nobody is photographing your feet" and mean it. if you want a matching accessory, buy it yourself and gift it. it doubles as the thank-you gift you were going to buy anyway.

### 03. travel and accommodation

flights plus hotel for a destination weekend can hit $800+, and $800 is the polite version. a three-night minimum at the resort block, a flight in peak season, a rental car share, airport transfers, and food for a weekend where nearly every meal is out. for a bridesmaid who also has to travel to the bachelorette in a different city, you've now asked her to fund two trips for your wedding.

this is also where the hidden asks pile up. the welcome dinner she's expected at. the day-after brunch. the group activity on saturday morning. each one is another $40-80 that wasn't in the estimate she made in her head when she said yes.

**what you can do:** be blunabout the true minimum number of nights required, and say out loud which events are optional. if you're picking a destination, the single kindest thing you can do is book the bachelorette locally. if you can cover accommodation for your party, even two of the nights, do it and tell them early so they can plan.

### 04. shower and bachelorette

each bridesmaid's share averages $200-400 per event. two events, and that's $400-800 per person, before anyone buys a gift.

the mechanics are what make this brutal. the party splits the cost of the guest of honor, so every add-on you approve gets divided among fewer people than you think. a $600 airbnb across five bridesmaids is $120 each plus your share covered. add a $300 private chef, matching pajamas, a photographer for two hours, and a bar tab, and you are well past $400 a head without anyone making a single unreasonable decision.

**what you can do:** give your party a budget ceiling before they start planning, in a number, out loud. "please keep this under $250 per person including my share." they will be relieved. a one-night stay in a city people can drive to, with one nice dinner, is genuinely more fun than a four-day itinerary that quietly excludes whoever couldn't afford it.

### 05. the gifts

a wedding gift plus a bridal shower gift is easily $150-250. wedding party members tend to give more than the average guest because they feel closer to you and more visible. that's on top of everything above.

**what you can do:** say, once, clearly, that their presence is the gift and you are not tracking who gave what. some people will give anyway. say it anyway.

### 06. time off

fittings, rehearsal, wedding day. that's 3-5 days of PTO, and for anyone hourly, contract, or self-employed, PTO is not a policy, it's lost income. a bridesmaid who makes $25/hour and takes four days off has absorbed another $800 that will never appear in any wedding budget spreadsheet.

**what you can do:** batch things. one fitting instead of two. schedule the rehearsal late afternoon so people can work a half day. don't add a friday "setup day" that quietly becomes mandatory. tell people the earliest they can arrive and still be fine, not the earliest you'd like them there.

## add it up

low end, local wedding, generous couple:

- dress plus alterations: $150
- shoes and accessories: $80
- travel: $0 (everyone's local)
- one combined shower and bach: $200
- gifts: $150
- PTO: 3 days

roughly **$580 and 3 days off.**

high end, destination weekend, two separate events:

- dress plus alterations: $500
- shoes and accessories: $150
- travel and accommodation: $800+
- shower and bach: $400 each, so $800
- gifts: $250
- PTO: 5 days

roughly **$2,500 and a week of leave**, for one wedding. if your friend is in three weddings that year, you can see the problem.

## watch for these red flags in your own planning

these are the patterns that turn a generous yes into a quiet resentment:

- **the drip.** no single ask is unreasonable, but the asks keep coming for eleven months. a $60 activity here, a $45 brunch there. people can budget for a number. they cannot budget for an open tab.
- **"optional" that isn't.** if the whole party is going and there are photos, it's mandatory. either fund it, cut it, or say clearly that skipping it is fine and then act like it's fine.
- **assuming everyone's finances match yours.** the friend who never complains is often the one stretching hardest. she isn't going to tell you.
- **letting the maid of honor absorb everything.** she's frequently paying double, covering your share, and fronting deposits on a personal credit card. check on her specifically.
- **announcing the destination before asking.** if the venue is already booked and non-refundable, "will you be my bridesmaid?" is not really a question.
- **discovering your own budget too late.** if you don't know what your wedding actually costs, you can't know what you're able to absorb for other people. that's the part most couples get backwards.

## the short version

before you send a single proposal box:

1. **do the math.** $1,400-2,500 is the honest range for a destination weekend. know your number before you ask.
2. **cover one thing, fully.** the dress plus alterations is the highest-impact $150-500 you can spend.
3. **host locally where you can.** skipping a destination bachelorette saves each person $800+.
4. **give the party a budget ceiling in dollars.** not vibes. a number.
5. **kill the mandatories.** coordinated shoes and jewelry is $80-150 you can hand back instantly.
6. **batch the days.** one fitting, a late rehearsal, no fake setup day. protect their PTO.
7. **ask before you book.** so the yes is a real yes.

then ask. most people will say yes and mean it. they'll just mean it a lot more when you've already thought about what you're asking.
