your glam quote says $1,450 for the bride, four bridesmaids, and mom. hair and makeup for everyone. you screenshot it, you send it to the group chat, you slot $1,450 into the spreadsheet and move on to florals.

then the invoice lands the week of the wedding and it says $2,180. and there is a note at the bottom asking you to bring gratuity in cash, in separate envelopes, for three artists.

nothing shady happened. every one of those charges was probably in the contract. they just were not in the proposal, and the proposal is the thing you actually read.

your hair and makeup quote looks clean. then the day arrives. travel mileage per artist each way, a trial at nearly full price, an early-morning surcharge if your getting-ready timeline starts before 7am, gratuity in cash for every person touching your hair or face, a minimum headcount just to get the team to your venue, and an hourly rate if you want anyone to stay through the reception.

none of those lines show up in the original proposal. they show up when you are already committed, already excited, and not really in a position to negotiate.

here are the six, what they typically run, and the exact question to ask before you sign.

## 1. travel fee: $1.50-$3 per mile each way, per artist

this is the one that quietly doubles. travel is usually billed at $1.50-$3 per mile each way, per artist. read that again: each way, and per artist.

so if your venue is 40 miles from your artist's home base and you have booked a team of three, the math is not 40 miles. it is 40 miles, doubled for the round trip, multiplied by three artists. at $2 a mile that is $480 in travel on a service you thought cost $1,450.

it gets bigger if your team is coming from a different city, because then you may also be looking at a hotel room night, parking, or tolls. some contracts fold those in as a flat "travel and accommodation" line, which is honestly easier to budget. some itemize everything after the fact.

**what to ask:** is travel billed per artist or per team? is it round trip? what is the origin address you are measuring from? (that last one matters. an artist may live an hour from the studio address on their website.)

### the fix that actually works

get the getting-ready location locked before you book glam, not after. couples who book glam first and then move getting-ready to an airbnb 30 minutes further out eat the difference. if you are still deciding between a hotel suite and the venue's bridal suite, price glam travel for both.

## 2. trial session: usually 75-100% of the day-of rate

most people assume the trial is a discounted preview. it is usually 75-100% of the day-of rate, and often it is billed as a completely separate service.

so if bridal hair and makeup on the day is $350, your trial is likely $260-$350. if you do a hair trial and a makeup trial with two different artists, you are paying two of those. if you travel to their studio, you are paying for your own gas and probably a weekday off work. if they come to you, travel mileage applies to the trial too.

this is not a scam. a trial takes the same two hours and the same product as the wedding day, and it is genuinely how you avoid looking like a stranger in your own photos. but it needs to sit in your budget as its own number, not as a rounding error.

**what to ask:** what is the trial rate, is it credited toward the balance if I book, and how many looks does it include? some artists include one look and charge for a second. if you are torn between a sleek low bun and something softer, that is a second look.

## 3. early call time: before 7am can add $50-$150 per artist

your photographer wants detail shots at 9am. your ceremony is at 1pm. you have six people in hair and makeup. work backward and your start time is 6:30am.

an early call before 7am can add $50-$150 per artist. three artists at the high end is $450 for the privilege of starting half an hour earlier than the artist's standard window.

the frustrating part is that early call times are almost always caused by something other than glam: a first-look slot, a shuttle schedule, a church that will not move. so the fee shows up after your timeline is already locked by three other vendors.

**what to ask:** what time does the standard rate begin, and what is the surcharge threshold? then build your timeline with that number visible. sometimes adding one more artist costs less than pushing everyone's start time earlier, because a fourth pair of hands compresses the schedule. sometimes it costs more. you can only compare if you know both rates.

## 4. gratuity: 20-25% per artist, in cash, day of

gratuity for glam runs 20-25% per artist, in cash, day of. it is one of the few wedding tips that is close to universally expected, because the work is personal service, hands-on, hours long, and performed in your hotel room at 6am.

on a $1,450 service that is $290-$362. and it is per artist, meaning you need it split into envelopes, labeled, and handed off by someone who is not you. you will be in a robe getting your lashes applied.

the failure mode here is not the cost, it is the logistics. people forget that cash means cash. atm withdrawal limits are real. the morning of your wedding is a bad time to find out yours is $300.

1. confirm the expected gratuity percentage in writing when you book
2. ask whether a service charge on the invoice already includes gratuity (sometimes it does, and double-tipping is a $300 mistake)
3. get the final artist count two weeks out, not two days out
4. withdraw the cash at least a week ahead
5. label one envelope per artist with their name and the amount
6. hand the envelopes to your planner, your MOH, or whoever is holding your phone

## 5. minimum headcount: most kits require 4-6 people to travel

this is the one that surprises solo brides the hardest. most kits require 4-6 people to travel. meaning the artist will not load the kit, drive to your venue, and do one face. the day is blocked either way, so there is a floor.

if you are the only person getting glam, you will likely be charged for the minimum anyway, or charged a "single service on-location" premium. if your mom decides two weeks out that she would rather do her own makeup, you may still be paying for her slot because the minimum was set at booking.

it also cuts the other way. if you have eight people, ask what happens above the minimum, because some artists bring a second artist automatically at a certain count and that changes travel, gratuity, and early call math all at once.

**what to ask:** what is the on-location minimum, is it a headcount or a dollar amount, and what is the cancellation window for individual services? a bridesmaid backing out of hair three days before should not cost you full price, but sometimes it does.

## 6. staying on-site past the ceremony: $75-$200/hr

you want someone there to fix your lipstick after the ceremony, redo a curl that dropped, or switch you from veil to comb before the reception. that is a real service and it is not included.

staying on-site past the ceremony adds $75-$200/hr. if you want an artist through cocktail hour and into the first dance, that is three or four hours. at the high end you are looking at $600-$800.

the cheaper version: ask for a touch-up kit instead. many artists will leave you a small bag with your exact lip color, blotting papers, and pins for a flat fee, or free if you ask nicely at booking. it handles 80% of what you actually need. the full stay-on-site rate is worth it if you have an outfit change, a cultural ceremony with multiple looks, or an outdoor event in august humidity.

## watch for these red flags

- a proposal with a single total and no itemization. if you cannot see the line items, you cannot see the add-ons
- "travel billed at cost" with no rate or origin address named
- no written artist count. "my team" is not a number, and gratuity and travel both scale with it
- a trial that is not mentioned at all in the contract, which usually means it is priced separately later
- no surcharge threshold listed for early start times
- a contract that references an attached rate sheet you were never sent
- pressure to sign before your getting-ready location is confirmed

none of these mean the artist is dishonest. glam pricing is genuinely complicated and most artists are small businesses using a template. it just means the number in your spreadsheet is wrong until you ask.

## the real total, and how to get to it

the fix is reading the contract before you sign it, not after. knowing these six line items going in means you can ask the right questions, budget the real total, and avoid the scramble for cash on the morning of.

before you sign:

1. get travel quoted per artist, round trip, from a named origin address
2. ask the trial rate and whether it credits toward your balance
3. confirm the early call threshold and the per-artist surcharge
4. put 20-25% gratuity in the budget line, not in the "we'll figure it out" pile
5. ask the on-location minimum and the per-service cancellation window
6. decide now whether you want an artist on-site after the ceremony at $75-$200/hr, or a touch-up kit instead

then add all of it up and compare that number against the other quotes you are holding. the artist with the higher base rate and no travel fee is often cheaper than the one with the friendly headline number and 40 miles between you.
