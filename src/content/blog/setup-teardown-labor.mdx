a couple books 120 chiavari chairs, eight farm tables, a dance floor, and a ceremony arch. the rental quote says $4,200 and they budget $4,200. the final invoice says $6,150.

nothing was added. no one upgraded anything. the difference was labor: a four-hour crew minimum for a team that was on site for 50 minutes, an install rate for the arch build, a same-night strike surcharge because the venue required everything gone by 1am, and a scheduling fee because a second wedding was loading in the next morning.

the quote covers the rentals. it rarely covers the people who show up at 7am to set them and midnight to break them down. here are the six labor lines that show up most often, what they typically cost, and which vendor actually sends the bill.

## 1. the crew minimum

most rentals require a 3-4 hour minimum per crew member, even for a 45-minute install. it bills regardless of actual time on site.

this is the one couples find hardest to accept, because it feels like being charged for time nobody worked. from the vendor's side the math is simple: a two-person crew drives 40 minutes each way, loads, unloads, stages, and can't book another job in that window. the minimum protects their day.

what matters for your budget is that the minimum is per person, not per job. a four-person crew on a three-hour minimum is twelve billable hours, not three. if your install genuinely takes 45 minutes, you are paying for roughly eleven hours of crew time you never see.

**who sends the bill:** the rental company or florist, usually as a line called "labor," "install," or "crew" on the final invoice, not the deposit invoice.

**what to ask before you sign:**
1. is the minimum per crew member or per crew?
2. how many people will be on the install crew for my specific order?
3. does the minimum apply separately to delivery and pickup, or does one minimum cover both?
4. if my order shrinks between now and the final count, does the crew size (and therefore the minimum) shrink with it?

that last question matters. plenty of couples cut their guest count by 20 and never think to ask whether the crew size was recalculated.

## 2. the install rate

florists and rental companies charge $50-150 per hour per installer for setup beyond standard drop-off. ask who pays if install runs long.

"standard drop-off" is narrower than most people picture. it usually means the vendor brings items to a single loading point and leaves. everything past that is install: carrying chairs upstairs, placing tables to a floor plan, hanging anything, building anything, ladder work, lighting, or draping.

floral installs are where this escalates fastest. a hanging installation over the head table, an arch that gets rebuilt at the reception, or anything that requires two people and a lift is billed by the hour per person. at $150 an hour with three installers, a four-hour build is $1,800 in labor on top of the flowers themselves.

### the "runs long" clause

the question nobody asks is what happens when install takes longer than estimated, and the reason it takes longer is usually not the vendor's fault. the venue wasn't ready. the previous event hadn't cleared. the ceremony site was still wet. the lift didn't fit through the door.

some contracts cap overage at the estimated hours. most don't. get it in writing: who absorbs the cost if install runs long, and is there a ceiling? if the answer is "we bill actual hours," ask for a written not-to-exceed number.

**who sends the bill:** the florist or rental company, typically added post-event as "additional install hours."

## 3. the strike surcharge

same-night teardown after 10pm often triggers a premium, commonly 1.5x the standard hourly rate. overnight pickup can cost more than the rentals.

strike is the reverse of install, and it happens at the worst possible hour for labor pricing. if your reception ends at 11pm and the venue requires the space clear by 1am, your crew is working a 1.5x window for two hours minimum, on top of the crew minimum from line one.

the alternative, next-morning pickup, is sometimes cheaper and sometimes not. it depends entirely on whether the venue allows items to stay overnight, and whether they charge you for that privilege. some venues do both: no overnight storage, and no strike access before 8am the following day. that combination is what forces same-night strike at premium rates.

### run the comparison before you pick a package

ask both vendors, rental and venue, the same three questions:

1. what is the hard deadline for the space to be clear?
2. can rentals stay overnight, and is there a storage fee?
3. what is the strike rate before 10pm versus after 10pm?

then price both paths. a same-night strike at 1.5x for a four-person crew can easily run more than the difference between two venue packages.

**who sends the bill:** the rental company or florist for the labor, and sometimes the venue separately for after-hours building access.

## 4. venue-mandated labor

some venues mandate their own union or in-house crew for all setup, at $75-200 per hour per worker. your planner and rental crew cannot substitute for them.

this one surprises people because it duplicates labor you already paid for. your rental company brings a crew. the venue says that crew can deliver to the dock but cannot touch anything past it. venue staff, billed separately, do the actual placement.

it's common in hotels, museums, historic properties, and any venue with a union agreement or an insurance policy that restricts who can work on site. it is not negotiable in most cases, and it is almost never listed in the venue's headline pricing.

there is a real planning consequence too: your planner cannot step in to speed things up. if you've been picturing your coordinator moving chairs during the ceremony-to-reception flip, and the venue mandates in-house labor, that flip is billed by the hour per worker.

**who sends the bill:** the venue, usually on a post-event statement or applied against your final balance.

**ask before you book:** is outside labor permitted for setup and strike? if not, what is the hourly rate, the minimum number of workers, and the minimum number of hours? get the estimate in writing based on your actual floor plan, not a generic number.

## 5. early access

venues typically grant access 2-4 hours before the event. anything earlier costs $150-500 per hour. florists and rental teams need more time than you think.

four hours sounds generous until you sequence it: rentals deliver and place, then linens go on, then the florist works, then lighting, then the planner styles tables, then a walkthrough. large floral installs alone can need six to eight hours. anything involving a lift, draping, or a ceremony-to-reception flip needs more.

at $500 an hour, three extra hours is $1,500. that is a real line item, and it is almost always discovered late, because the timeline doesn't get built until after the venue contract is signed.

the fix is sequencing the day before you sign. ask your florist and rental company how many hours they need for your specific scope. then compare that to the access window in the venue contract. if there's a gap, you either pay for it, cut the scope, or choose a different venue.

## 6. the delivery window fee

tight delivery windows or back-to-back bookings add a scheduling fee, often $100-300, billed by the vendor. ask what happens if the venue runs behind.

if the venue gives your rental company a 90-minute slot because there's an event the night before and another the next morning, the vendor has to staff up to hit it. more people, less flexibility, a scheduling fee.

back-to-back bookings are also the single most common reason install runs long, which loops straight back to the install rate in section two. the venue running behind is not your vendor's problem contractually, which means it becomes yours.

## red flags in the contract

watch for these while you're reading:

- **"labor billed at prevailing rates"** with no number attached. ask for the number in writing.
- **a quote with no labor line at all.** it isn't free. it's unquoted, and it will appear on the final invoice.
- **"delivery included"** without a definition of delivery. included delivery usually means drop-off only.
- **no stated access window** in the venue contract, or a window described as "day of" without hours.
- **strike responsibility left vague.** if the contract doesn't say who breaks down and removes what, someone will bill you for it.
- **overtime language that references the venue's schedule, not yours.** that's how a delayed ceremony turns into a 1.5x strike.
- **separate deposit and final invoices** where labor only appears on the final. ask to see a sample final invoice from a comparable event.

## before you sign, do these six things

1. ask every rental and floral vendor whether the crew minimum is per person or per crew, and how many people are on your crew.
2. get the install rate ($50-150 per hour per installer is the normal range) and a written answer on who pays if install runs long.
3. price same-night strike against next-morning pickup, including any overnight storage fee.
4. confirm in writing whether the venue mandates in-house labor at $75-200 per hour, and get an estimate for your floor plan.
5. compare your florist's actual install time to the venue's 2-4 hour access window before you sign the venue contract.
6. ask about scheduling fees ($100-300) and what happens if the venue runs behind.

none of these fees are scams. crews are real people doing physical work at unpleasant hours, and they deserve to be paid for it. the problem is that the numbers show up after the decision, when you have no leverage left. move them earlier and they're just budget.
