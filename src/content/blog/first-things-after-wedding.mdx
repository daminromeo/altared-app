say the last song ends, somebody hands you a to-go box of cake, and you wake up the next morning in a hotel room with a dress bag on the chair and absolutely no idea what you're supposed to do first. the ceremony ends and then... nobody tells you what happens next. here it is, in order.

this list exists because the week after goes fast and none of this is automatic. nothing here takes long on its own. what gets people is the order, and the fact that two of these six items have deadlines attached that you did not set and cannot move.

## why order matters more than speed

most post-wedding tasks are not urgent in the emergency sense. they're urgent in the "this gets 4x harder in three weeks" sense. tips are easiest to hand over while your vendors are still in the room. reviews are easiest to write while you can still picture the moment. your name change has a sequence baked into it, and doing it out of order means redoing parts of it.

so the sequence below is not arbitrary. it moves from "do this today" to "do this before the quarter ends," and each step clears the way for the next one.

## 1. tip your vendors within 24 hours

tip your team within 24 hours, cash or venmo, before memory fades.

the cleanest version of this is to prep labeled envelopes before the wedding and hand them to whoever is running your day, so tips go out during breakdown. if that didn't happen, venmo works and it works fine. send it the next morning while you still remember who stayed late, who fixed something quietly, and who went off-script to help.

a few things worth knowing:

1. check your contracts first. some vendors include a service charge or gratuity line, and you do not need to tip twice on the same line.
2. tip the people who worked the day, not just the people who signed the contract. second shooters, assistants, catering staff, the band's sound tech.
3. if cash is going out in envelopes, write the person's name and role on the front. nobody wants to guess.
4. send it from one person, not both of you separately. duplicate venmos are a very normal newlywed mistake.

if you're reading this before the wedding and you want to see which of your vendors already has gratuity baked into their quote, that's exactly the kind of line item worth tracking in one place. you can [add your quotes free](/get-started) and compare the all-in numbers side by side.

## 2. mail thank you notes within 3 months

mail within 3 months. make them specific.

three months is the window. that's generous, and it's also exactly why people miss it. the trick is not writing faster, it's writing shorter and more specific.

a good opening line does most of the work: "the [gift] is already in our home and we love it." it tells the person their gift arrived, that you know what it was, and that it's being used. everything after that can be two sentences.

### how to not lose the thread

- keep one running list of who gave what, started the day gifts begin arriving, not after the wedding.
- for cash or checks, name what it's going toward rather than the amount.
- for guests who traveled, thank them for the trip, not just the gift.
- split the stack by who knows whom. you write your side, your partner writes theirs, and neither of you stalls on a name you can't place.

## 3. start your name change with social security

social security first, then DMV.

if you're changing your name, the order genuinely matters. social security first, then the DMV, then everything downstream (passport, bank, employer, insurance, airline loyalty accounts). doing the DMV first often means going back, because state systems check against social security records that haven't updated yet.

you'll need a certified copy of your marriage certificate for most of this, which is why the next step and this one are linked. fees for the certified copy vary by county. processing times vary too, so check your county before you book a day off work for it.

order more than one certified copy while you're at it. several agencies want to see an original, and a few will hold onto it.

## 4. leave vendor reviews while the details are fresh

leave reviews while details are fresh. say one specific thing.

generic five-star reviews help nobody, including the vendor. one concrete detail is worth three paragraphs of "amazing, highly recommend." something like: "she spotted our timeline was off by 20 minutes."

that level of specificity does two things. it tells the next couple what this person is actually good at, and it gives the vendor something they can point to. if you found your vendors through reviews (most people do), this is the part where you put something back into the system you used.

post on the platforms where you actually found them, and send the vendor a note that the review is up. small thing, goes a long way.

## 5. confirm your officiant filed your marriage license

this is the one that quietly matters most. confirm your officiant filed it.

a signed license sitting in somebody's car is not a marriage record. the officiant has to return it to the issuing office, and the office has to record it. then, and only then, can you order a certified copy.

so: text your officiant, confirm it was filed and when, then request a certified copy now. processing time varies by county, so if you have a honeymoon with international travel or a name change deadline tied to a passport, start early rather than assuming it'll be ready.

### watch for these red flags

- **your officiant goes quiet after the wedding.** one unanswered text is fine. three is a problem. call the county clerk directly and ask whether your license has been recorded.
- **the county has no record weeks later.** licenses have return deadlines. if yours was missed, the clerk will tell you what the fix is, and the fix gets harder the longer you wait.
- **nobody checked the signatures on the day.** missing witness lines, a wrong date, an unsigned block. these get the license rejected and sent back, and you will not hear about it unless you ask.
- **you're told "it's all set" with no filing date.** ask for the date it was returned. "all set" is not a date.
- **you only ordered one certified copy.** not a crisis, just a guaranteed second trip.

## 6. send your guests a note within 48 hours

text or post within 48 hours: "we are still smiling. thank you for being there."

this is separate from thank you notes and it serves a different purpose. thank you notes are for gifts. this is for presence. people took time off, booked flights, found a sitter, and then went home to silence.

one group text, one story, one post. short is correct here. then send your guests a quick note within 48 hours so they know you felt them there.

## the short version

1. tip your vendors within 24 hours, cash or venmo.
2. mail thank you notes within 3 months, and make them specific.
3. start your name change with social security, then the DMV.
4. leave vendor reviews while you still remember the exact moments that mattered.
5. confirm your officiant filed your license, then order a certified copy, knowing fees and processing times vary by county.
6. send guests a note within 48 hours.
