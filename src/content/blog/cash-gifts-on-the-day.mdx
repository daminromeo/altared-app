say the reception is in full swing. dinner is cleared, the first dance just ended, and the card table is sitting by the entrance exactly where the planner set it up at 4pm. it looks sweet. it is also completely unattended for most of the night, and everyone in the room knows what's in it.

nobody plans for cash to disappear. that is sort of the point. the gift table is one of the only parts of a wedding day with real money sitting on it and no one specifically assigned to watch it, because the people who would normally notice (you, your parents, your wedding party) are all busy doing wedding things. the fix is not paranoia. it is four small decisions you make before the day, written down somewhere other than your brain.

## 1. one named person, assigned in advance

the most common version of this plan is "my mom will grab the cards." that is not a plan, that is a hope. assign one person by name before the day, not whoever's nearby at the card table.

a named holder means you have asked a specific human, out loud, in advance, and they said yes. they know it is a job. they know when it starts and when it ends. a volunteer who gets handed the box at 11pm while holding a drink and a toddler is not the same thing.

who to ask:

1. someone who is not in the wedding party, or at least not in photos all night
2. someone who is not drinking much, or not at all
3. someone who has a car at the venue, or access to a room with a lock
4. someone who will still be there at the end of the night, not leaving early with the kids
5. someone you can text directly, who will actually read the text

one person. not a committee. the second you split the job between "mom and my maid of honor," each one assumes the other has it. name one, and tell a backup that they are the backup, so there is no confusion about who is in charge.

### tell the venue and the planner who it is

your coordinator should know the name too. if a staff member sees someone walking out with the card box, the only thing standing between that and a very bad night is whether anyone knows who is supposed to be carrying it. say the name out loud at the rehearsal.

## 2. a card box that actually locks

a card box with a lock costs little, and only your holder has the key. that is the whole upgrade. a decorative birdcage with a ribbon is a container, not security, because anyone can lift the lid or lift the entire cage.

what to look for:

- a real lock or latch, not a magnetic closure
- a slot sized for envelopes but too small for a hand
- weight, or something to secure it to the table, so it is not a grab-and-go item
- a key that lives with your named holder from the start of cocktail hour, not taped to the bottom of the box

the key detail matters more than the box. if the key is in a drawer at the venue or in your clutch in the bridal suite, you have a locked box with no one able to open it and a holder who now has to carry an awkward sealed container around looking for you. give the key to the holder when you give them the job.

### if you are using a basket or a bowl

some couples want the open basket look for photos. that is fine for cocktail hour if someone is standing near it, but it should get swapped for the locked box before guests sit down for dinner. decide that swap in advance and put it on the timeline, otherwise it does not happen.

## 3. move the box mid-reception, not at the end

this is the one most people get backwards. during dinner, your holder moves the box to a locked car or room, not after.

the end of the night is the worst possible time to handle money. people are tired, a little drunk, the venue is kicking everyone out, vendors are loading out through the same doors, and the lights are down. the box gets set on a chair "for one second" while someone grabs the leftover cake, and that second is the whole problem.

dinner is the opposite of that. guests are seated and accounted for, the room is calm, the lighting is up, and nobody is watching the door. your holder slips out, puts the box in a locked car or a locked room, and comes back before dessert. pick one spot and say it out loud: a specific car, a specific room, locked.

a few things to decide in advance:

1. exactly where the box goes (which car, which room, who else has access)
2. exactly when it moves, tied to a timeline moment, not a clock time you might run late on
3. whether any late-arriving cards need a second trip, and who handles those
4. who has the box at the end of the night and where it sleeps

if cards keep arriving after dinner, leave a smaller second box out and have your holder do one more sweep at a set point. that is still better than leaving everything out until midnight.

## 4. put the plan in writing

text your holder the plan in writing: "take the box to mom's car by 8pm." that is the entire step. it takes thirty seconds and it removes memory from the equation on a very long day.

written beats verbal because on the wedding day, you will say roughly four hundred things to forty people and remember almost none of it. a text sits in their phone. they can check it at 7:50pm when they cannot find you, because you are taking photos in a field.

a good handoff text includes the person's job, the location, the timing trigger, and what happens at the end of the night. keep it short enough to read on a lock screen. if your holder is handing the box to someone else later (say, your parents taking it home), name that person in the same text so there is no second conversation required.

this is the same logic as the rest of your day-of paperwork: the plan only works if it exists somewhere outside your head. if you are already keeping your vendor contacts and quotes in one place, add the card box plan to it. you can [add your quotes free](/get-started) and keep the day-of details next to the contracts they belong with.

## watch for these

- a card box with no lock, or a lock whose key nobody can find
- "someone will grab it" as the entire plan
- a holder who is also in every photo, giving a speech, and driving people home
- the box moving only after the last song, in the dark, during load-out
- the key taped to the bottom of the box (this happens more than you would think)
- a venue that says they will "keep it behind the bar" with no named staff member attached
- guests who hand envelopes directly to you during the reception, which then live in a jacket pocket or a clutch that gets set down somewhere

that last one is worth a sentence to your holder: if anyone hands you cards, they go straight in the box, not in a pocket.

## the short version

1. name one person in advance and ask them directly, not whoever's nearby at the card table
2. use a card box with a lock and give the key only to that person
3. move the box during dinner to a locked car or room, not after the dancing
4. text the plan in writing so nothing relies on memory at the end of a very long day
