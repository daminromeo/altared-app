say three things land in the same week: a package from your aunt that arrives the day after you get engaged, a check tucked into a card from your boss, and a box from the registry that shows up while you're still unpacking from the honeymoon. all three are gifts. all three need a note. and none of them are due at the same time.

that's the part nobody tells you. the thank-you note timeline is not one deadline. it's six different ones, and each one starts the clock at a different moment. the clock starts when the gift reaches you, not when the wedding happens, which is why a single "send thank-yous after the wedding" rule falls apart the second gifts start arriving in a staggered way (and they always do).

here are the six windows, what starts each one, and a line you can borrow for each.

## the six deadlines, in order

1. **engagement gift**: send within 2 weeks of receiving it.
2. **shower gift**: 2 weeks after the shower, no later.
3. **pre-wedding cash**: write it before the wedding, mail it after.
4. **day-of gift**: note within 2 weeks of returning from honeymoon.
5. **late arrival**: gift arrives after you're home, send within 2 weeks of it.
6. **vendor gift**: a handwritten note within a week, separate from the tip.

six windows, six different starting guns. the deadlines are the ones etiquette experts have agreed on for decades, so there's no debate to have here. the only real work is knowing which window a gift falls into the moment it shows up.

## the three pre-wedding windows

### 01. engagement gift

people send things the week you get engaged, sometimes before you've told everyone. send within 2 weeks of receiving it. the two weeks start the day it lands on your porch, not the day you finally open the pile.

borrow this: *"we love this so much, thank you for celebrating with us."*

short is fine here. an engagement gift note is not the place to write a paragraph. the sender wants to know it arrived and that you liked it.

### 02. shower gift

2 weeks after the shower, no later. this one has a clean start date, which makes it the easiest of the six to hit and the most obvious when you miss it. everyone at that shower knows the date it happened.

borrow this: *"you made that afternoon feel so special for me."*

the shower note works best when it's about the person and not just the object. whoever hosted gets a note too, even if they didn't bring a wrapped gift. hosting is the gift.

### 03. pre-wedding cash

cash and checks that arrive before the day get their own rule: write it before the wedding, mail it after. this sounds fussy until you've done it. writing in the calm weeks before the wedding means the note actually gets written, and mailing after means you're not thanking someone for a wedding gift before the wedding has happened.

borrow this: *"this will go straight toward our honeymoon, thank you."*

naming where the money is going is the whole point of a cash note. "thank you for the generous gift" reads like a form letter. telling someone it's funding the honeymoon, or the deposit, or the new kitchen, makes it specific.

## the two post-wedding windows

### 04. day-of gift

anything handed to you on the day: note within 2 weeks of returning from honeymoon. the clock starts when you're home, not when you got married. this is the deadline most people silently extend to "sometime this fall" and then "around the holidays."

borrow this: *"having you there meant everything to us."*

for guests who traveled, add one line about them being there. for everyone else, name the gift. the two together take about ninety seconds per card.

### 05. late arrival

gifts that show up after you're home get their own window: send within 2 weeks of it. not two weeks from the wedding, two weeks from the box.

borrow this: *"your gift arrived and made us smile all over again."*

this one matters more than it looks. a late gift usually comes from someone who was worried about getting it right, or who shipped it to the wrong address first. they are waiting to find out it made it.

## 06. the one people forget: vendors

a handwritten note within a week, separate from the tip. this is not a thank-you for a gift, it's a thank-you for work, and it's the single highest-leverage note you'll write. vendors keep these. they also write reviews, refer you, and remember you when you need a last-minute favor or a copy of a file three years later.

borrow this: *"you brought exactly the vision we had in our heads."*

separate from the tip is the operative phrase. a tip in an envelope with no note is a transaction. a note with no tip (where a tip is expected) is a miss. do both, in the same week, while the specifics are still fresh enough for you to name one thing they actually did. if you're still working out who gets tipped and how much, read up before the week of in [contracts](/blog/category/contracts) and [budgeting](/blog/category/budgeting).

## watch for these

the deadlines aren't hard. the tracking is. here's where it usually breaks down:

- **the unlabeled gift.** a card separates from a box at the venue and now you have a gift with no sender and a sender with no gift. assign someone to keep cards with boxes on the day.
- **the group gift.** one check, five names. every name gets a note, not one note to the organizer.
- **"we'll do them together."** a pile split between two people who each think the other person is handling the top half. divide by name, in writing, before you start.
- **the shower gift opened at the shower but recorded nowhere.** someone needs to be writing down who gave what while it's being opened. that's a real job, assign it to a real person.
- **waiting for the "nice" stationery.** cards sitting in a cart for six weeks is worse than a plain note sent on time. buy something simple now.
- **thanking by text because it's faster.** a text is fine as an "it arrived!" note. it does not replace the handwritten one.

the common thread: miss one and it sits in your head for months. that's the actual cost. not etiquette points, just the low background hum of knowing you owe someone something.

## the system that makes this work

you don't need a complicated setup. you need one list, updated the day a gift arrives, with four columns: who, what, which deadline window, sent yes/no. that's it.

write notes in small batches rather than one marathon session. five a sitting beats forty in one afternoon, and the notes read better. keep stamps and cards in one drawer so the only task is writing. and when a gift arrives, log it before you put the box down.

**quick recap:**

1. engagement gift: within 2 weeks of receiving it.
2. shower gift: 2 weeks after the shower, no later.
3. pre-wedding cash: write it before the wedding, mail it after.
4. day-of gift: within 2 weeks of returning from honeymoon.
5. late arrival: within 2 weeks of the gift arriving.
6. vendors: handwritten note within a week, separate from the tip.
7. log every gift the day it lands, with the sender's name attached.
