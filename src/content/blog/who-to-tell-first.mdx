say you get engaged on a Saturday morning. the ring is on, the photos are already in your camera roll, and your hands are shaking a little. by 10am you have texted four people, posted a story because you were excited, and by 10:04 your aunt has commented "WHAT!!!!" and your mom has seen it before you called her.

nothing is ruined. but that is the version of the story your mom will tell for the next thirty years.

the order you tell people matters more than you think. parents before siblings, siblings before friends, friends before the group chat, everyone before the post. finding out from instagram instead of a phone call is the thing people remember. the list is short and the calls are fast, but getting the sequence right means nobody feels like an afterthought.

here is the order, and why each spot is where it is.

## the order, start to finish

1. **your parents.** call before you post. same day, both sides.
2. **close siblings.** text or call within 24 hours, before the feed.
3. **best friends.** the ones who will be in it. voice memo, not a group chat.
4. **extended family.** grandparents and aunts before the announcement goes live.
5. **your boss.** within a week, before you request time off or a date change.
6. **the feed.** everyone else, once the calls are done.

that is it. six beats, most of them under five minutes each. you can do the whole top half of this list in one afternoon with a coffee and a charged phone.

## 01. your parents

call before you post. same day, both sides.

both sides is the part people drop. if you call your parents Saturday and your partner calls theirs Sunday "because of the time difference," one set of parents finds out a full day late, and they will know it. split the work: you call yours, your partner calls theirs, same afternoon, and you compare notes after so nobody is working from a different version of the story.

a few things worth deciding before you dial:

- **are you telling them the date?** you do not have one yet. say so plainly, or the first question becomes a planning conversation you are not ready for.
- **are you telling them about money?** also no. "we have not talked about any of that yet" is a complete sentence and it buys you weeks.
- **who is getting the first call if your parents are divorced or estranged?** decide that in advance, not while the phone is ringing.

### if a parent is far away or hard to reach

leave a voicemail that says call me back, not a text that says "guess what." then keep trying. a parent who finds out from a cousin because you gave up after one ring is a very fixable problem, and the fix is just more attempts.

## 02. close siblings

text or call within 24 hours, before the feed.

siblings are usually the easiest calls and the ones most likely to get bumped, because you assume they already know. they do not. a sibling who sees the ring on your story before you say anything directly is going to feel like a bystander in something they expected to be inside of.

if you have a sibling you are hoping will stand up with you, this call is not the ask. let the news land on its own. the ask deserves its own moment, and stacking the two means they are processing a job while they are still saying congratulations.

## 03. best friends

the ones who will be in it. voice memo, not a group chat.

this is the most underrated line on the list. a group chat announcement means the first person to reply sets the tone and everyone else reacts to the reaction. a voice memo is personal, it takes forty seconds, and they can listen to it in the car and scream alone, which is what they actually want to do.

keep this circle small on day one. "the ones who will be in it" is a useful filter, even if you have no idea yet who is standing where. you are not building a guest list, you are making four or five people feel chosen.

## 04. extended family

grandparents and aunts before the announcement goes live.

grandparents first, and by phone if they have one they answer. for the rest of the extended family, this is where delegation is legitimate: ask your mom to call her siblings, ask your partner's dad to handle his side. you are not required to personally dial twenty-two people in one weekend.

the only rule is that the delegating happens **before** the post, not after. once something is on the feed, everyone who hears it secondhand is in the secondhand group forever.

## 05. your boss

one thing that gets skipped: telling your boss within the first week. you will need time off, a date approved, and possibly a schedule shift. that conversation goes better when it is not a surprise.

you are not asking permission. you are putting a flag in the ground early so that when you do request time off, it is the second conversation instead of the first. say you are engaged, say you do not have a date yet, say you will bring dates as soon as you have them. that is the whole meeting.

if your job has blackout periods, a busy season, or a formal leave request window, now is when you find out, not three months after you have signed a venue contract. knowing your employer's constraints before you lock a date is one of the quietest ways to avoid an expensive problem later. if you want to see how date decisions ripple into everything else, our [planning](/blog/category/planning) posts go deeper on sequencing.

## 06. the feed

everyone else, once the calls are done.

post whenever you want. same day, next week, three months later. the only requirement is that it comes after the calls, so that nobody on the first five lines of this list is learning it from a caption.

## watch for these

a few things that reliably go sideways:

- **the accidental story.** you post a ring photo "just to close friends" and your close friends list has 180 people on it. check who is actually on it before you tap.
- **someone else posting first.** tell every person you call that you have not announced yet. one excited sibling can end your sequence in ninety seconds.
- **a photographer or venue tagging you.** if a proposal photographer was involved, ask them directly to hold the images until you say go. put it in writing.
- **the date question.** at least one person will push for a date on the first call. "we have not talked about any of that yet" works on all of them.
- **telling your boss by accident.** a coworker seeing the ring at Monday standup is not the version of that conversation you want.
